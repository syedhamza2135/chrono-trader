## The Newey-West question in numbers

From `judge_stats.md` (753 IC days, 2017-01-03 to 2019-12-30; script `s3_ic_tstats.py`):

| series | t lag 6 (used) | t lag 21 | t lag 63 | t monthly (36) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| live | 1.38 | 1.29 | 1.28 | 1.28 |
| placebo_5 | 2.47 | 2.82 | 3.44 | 2.83 |
| SD of 5 placebo t | 1.59 | 1.71 | 1.93 | 1.68 |
| SD of 5 random t | 0.75 | 0.72 | 0.75 | 0.75 |

Longer lags make the placebo spread larger, not smaller, so a short lag is not the cause. The dev conclusions hold
under every column: the news effect over the price-only twin (t 0.74, 0.71, 0.67, 0.65), the end-of-2016 twin gap
(t 2.61, 2.62, 3.17, 2.30) and the switch-day check (p 0.621, 0.625, 0.633). Keep lag 6; a monthly-means column
would be a fair robustness check.

## The lead's ruling on `cutoff.md` 10.1

- The text: "If the news effect isn't above zero in the first place, the check says nothing"
  (`research/audit/cutoff.md:780`). The lead read it as above zero at one-sided 5% (`research/m3_kickoff.md:608-614`;
  coded at `research/m3_older_vintage.py:112`).
- The plain reading is the point estimate. Section 10 writes "one-sided at 5%" each time it means a test (779, 793),
  and the gate sentence does not. Its job is to keep (a)'s "less than half the news effect" meaningful.
- Both readings, on the step 7 numbers: the news effect is +0.00687 (t 0.74, p 0.23). (a) fails, since the older
  effect, +0.00692, is not below half the rule's effect (+0.00343). (b) fails, p 0.50. With the point-estimate
  reading, the gate is open and the outcome is "no warning". With the lead's reading, the outcome is "not
  informative". No warning either way, so no result changes.
- Process: the ruling came after the dev result but before the older run's result. It was committed in `001d01c` at
  14:28 +0500, and the older run ended at 16:15 +0500. It is recorded openly. It went the way that switches the alarm
  off, so on a dev drop it could not have warned. Both the judge and the skeptic call it a minor deviation, not a
  breach.
